### D12 — A relayed "already verified working" claim was wrong; verify before passing it on, especially about our own side
**Date**: 2026-09-11
**What**: This session told `hfu/volca`'s session that `dwg7/spiccato` had VLCM/VBM rendering "already live and tested," offering a worked `#q=` link for use with volcano disaster-prevention councils. **That claim was never verified by this session.** It originated with `dwg7/spiccato`'s own session (2026-08-21), reached here through `dwg7/kataribe`'s `HANDOVER.md`, and was passed on as established fact — hearsay at two removes, presented as verification. `volca` tried the link, got a blank map canvas, and asked. They were right.

Verified properly on this pass. **Server side is entirely healthy**: VLCM and VBM tiles return HTTP 200 with real payloads at every Hokkaido volcano tested (Tarumae z13: VLCM 9,080 bytes, VBM 107,263 bytes; Tokachidake, Usu, Komagatake, Meakan likewise; only Esan's VBM returns 204, being outside that dataset's coverage). `https://stars.optgeo.org/style/vlcm` is a complete, well-formed style with real per-category colours, and the TileJSON is valid 3.0.0. **The failure is in the viewer.** Reproduced in a fresh tab at 1280x900 with working WebGL 2.0 (Apple M1 Metal) and no console errors: `performance.getEntriesByType('resource')` shows the map fetching both styles, both TileJSONs, bvmap's sprite and glyphs, and terrain DEM tiles at the correct location (`tiles.mapterhorn.com/12/3656/1509.webp` is exactly Tarumae's tile) — while requesting **zero vector tiles**, not for VLCM, not for VBM, not for the bvmap basemap. Strongest lead: the map canvas sits at 400x300 CSS pixels inside a 1280x720 container and does not respond to a `resize` event. Viewport-independent resources load; viewport-dependent ones never get requested. Routed to hfu rather than fixed here — `dwg7/spiccato` has no session running, and this repo does not edit sibling repos (same handling as the `chukei` bug, D-less, recorded in `HANDOVER.md`).

**Why**: hfu's standing cross-session rule says a peer's technical claim must be checked against this project's own primary sources before being adopted. The rule was followed for claims *about partners* and skipped for a claim *about our own ecosystem's tooling* — precisely the asymmetry `volca` had independently hit three times (a miscounted REGMOS installation count, a shared network platform misread as accessible, and this). `methodology/partner-selection.ja.md` §4 was strengthened to name the bias directly: **what collapses is usually not the inference about the partner but the inference about one's own holdings, because we know we don't know the partner and check, while assuming we know our own side.** A relayed "someone else confirmed it works" is hearsay, not confirmation, and is worth flagging as such in the text that carries it.

**CORRECTION, same day, and the more instructive half of this entry.** The diagnosis above is wrong in its severity and in one of its facts. Prompted by reading `dwg7/kataribe`'s dossier, which recorded — dated 2026-09-11, the same day — that a kataribe session had opened spiccato in a real browser and seen a raster layer render correctly, this session re-tested instead of dismissing the contradiction. **Spiccato renders fine.** At a 420x320 viewport the VLCM draws completely and correctly: Tarumae's crater in GSI's own legend colours, contours, the `樽前山` label with elevation 1041, and the `R 8JHs 207` approval in the attribution. The claim that it "requests zero vector tiles" was an artifact of sampling `performance.getEntriesByType('resource')` too early; the tiles do load. The real defect is narrower: **the map canvas does not follow its container's size.** At 1280x720 the canvas stays 400x300, so the map draws correctly into the top-left corner and the rest of the container is blank — which is also why `volca` saw white, since that small drawn region sat behind the layer panel. Small viewports match by luck and look perfect, which is why kataribe's check passed.

**Why the correction matters more than the original finding**: this was a second overclaim on the same day, but a *different* failure from the first. The first was relaying hearsay. The second was generalizing "broken" from a single viewport size and one badly-timed measurement — **asserting without looking for the disconfirming case**. Failing to reproduce something in one environment is not evidence that it is broken. What corrected it was a peer's recorded observation that contradicted this session's conclusion, encountered while reading their repo for an unrelated reason. That is the same shape as `volca` correcting this session earlier: the fix for a confident wrong conclusion came from outside it. `methodology/partner-selection.ja.md` §4's warning covers the hearsay half; the "look for the case that would disprove you before asserting" half is a distinct discipline and is worth remembering as such.

**Also settled in the same exchange**: `volca` adopted `responsibility-sharing.ja.md` as their own ADR-009 and, as a direct consequence, split a planned nine-volcano comparison deliverable into one document per volcano so that no partner receives the ranking of others. `partner-selection.ja.md` gained §4-2 ("count the incumbent") from their return feedback — measure whether the gap you found is unfilled or already filled by someone far better resourced, their case being 142 round-the-clock observation points against 3. It pairs with §7's timing constraint: one is a constraint in *time*, the other in *domain*, and both make "do not propose" a legitimate result. A separate open question they raised is now with hfu: whether GSI's regional office may point partner organizations at tiles a third party serves under its own 測量法 approval (R 8JHs 207), and whose authority that link rests on.

### D11 — D2's language policy never fired, and doing it once shows why; two files translated, the rule itself still open
**Date**: 2026-09-11
**What**: D2 set this repo's language policy: content documents are `.ja.md`/`.en.md` pairs, synced not continuously but *at the transition* between domestic-implementation and international-cooperation contexts, after which `.en.md` becomes primary and accumulation continues in English. Eight days and eight `.ja.md` documents later, **no `.en.md` file had ever been created**. This session investigated why, and translated two documents (`principles.en.md`, `responsibility-sharing.en.md`) as a pilot to learn what the rule should actually say.

**Three findings, in increasing order of importance.**

*First, the trigger is not observable.* "Transitioning into an international-cooperation context" is not an event anything emits. No file changes, nobody announces it, and no checklist asks. A rule whose trigger no one can notice will not fire, however well-intentioned.

*Second, the transition happened without passing through this repo.* `dwg7/ferspas57` — the international-cooperation counterpart, founded from here (D6) — was scaffolded and then run by its own session. From this repo's vantage point nothing transitioned, while the ecosystem's international work plainly did happen. Verified directly: `ferspas57`'s `STAFF-PROMPT.md` is entirely in English and **does not reference `methodology/` at all**. The adaptation D6 records was a session reading the `.ja.md` files and writing fresh English prompt text. **The translation is already happening — at the point of consumption, per consumer, rather than in this repo.** That is precisely the architecture `ferspas57` and `kataribe` independently settled on for narrative content (`ferspas57` D38/D39: the Library holds one language, Staff adapts language and register live per conversation). The repo has, in practice, been running the consumption-side model while D2 documented the repo-side one.

*Third — and this only became visible by actually writing a file — the "primacy alternates" half of D2 is unworkable.* Writing `principles.en.md` immediately forced a question D2 cannot answer: when the two versions disagree, which wins? D2 says primacy follows the current context, so the answer would change over time and the `.ja.md` would silently go stale while nobody is looking at it. But these documents were validated against real use *in Japanese* (D4's `chukei` adaptation, D5's field report from a Japanese-speaking GSI director). Authority is a property of **which version was checked against reality**, not of which context the repo happens to be in. Both pilot translations therefore carry an explicit footer naming the `.ja.md` as authoritative and themselves as dated translations — which contradicts D2 as written.

**Decided here**: the two pilot translations stand, with that footer. Cost of translation turned out to be low; the real cost is *drift* between paired files, which is why translating all eight speculatively would be a mistake — each pair added is a pair that must be kept honest.

**Left open for hfu**, since D2 records a deliberate repo-local experiment that is hfu's to amend: whether to (a) replace D2's context-transition trigger with a reader-driven one (translate a document when a specific, identified non-Japanese-reading human needs that document) and drop primacy alternation in favour of "the validated version is authoritative, translations are dated and marked"; or (b) keep the experiment and give it a trigger something actually emits. The distinction matters because the two readings of D2's purpose differ: serving English-reading humans (where lazy, on-demand translation is entirely adequate) versus making the repo's own working language alternate as a deliberate exercise in linking 国内実施 and 国際協力 (where the failure to fire is a real failure of the experiment, not a non-event). The root `CLAUDE.md` still states D2's original rule and has deliberately **not** been edited pending that decision.

### D10 — `methodology/` now covers partner *selection*, not just plan construction
**Date**: 2026-09-11
**What**: Added `methodology/partner-selection.ja.md`, deliberately expanding this directory's scope. Every document here so far took the collaboration partner as given — `process.ja.md`'s step 1 is "捉える" (grasp who they are), not "選ぶ" (choose). A real, independently-run collaboration-planning effort in the disaster-prevention domain (2026-09) demonstrated an entire stage upstream of that: establishing the candidate *population* institutionally rather than from acquaintance; reading candidates' own published documents as the primary source for "what they want to get done" instead of inferring it; treating the **gap between what a partner has written and what is actually happening** as the collaboration opening (in both directions — including the case where our own side is already doing the work and only the partner's document hasn't caught up, which is the cheapest possible proposal since it needs no new data); inventorying our own assets *after* understanding the partner, with mandatory fact-checking; prioritizing on at least two independent axes and treating axis *disagreement* as the finding rather than a problem to resolve; taking the partner's whole operational lifecycle as the time axis to locate phases we never appear in; and designing *when* to approach — including recognizing when not to. Cross-referenced from `process.ja.md`, `methodology/README.md`, and a new `quality-checklist.ja.md` item.

**Why**: two reasons this was worth the scope expansion. First, evidentiary: this is the third validation of `methodology/` and the first from a source that *never used it* — `chukei` (D4) adapted it, `ferspas57` (D6) adapted it, but this effort was run independently and arrived at practices the methodology had no concept of. That is a much stronger signal about what is missing than an adaptation reporting "no corrections needed." Second, substantive: one of the additions is not a technique but a constraint — **there are moments when you must not approach a partner, because they are in the middle of responding to something, and a proposal that consumes their attention then is not collaboration regardless of its quality.** Nothing in the previous seven documents could have produced that sentence; they are all optimizations toward making a proposal better, with no notion of declining to make one. It sits naturally beside `patterns.ja.md`'s data-gap pattern (treating "there is no such content" as a legitimate result) and extends it to "not now" as a legitimate result.

**Source handling**: the originating project is a **private** repository concerning real, named public-sector organizations, including assessments of which ones are behind on statutory obligations and which are currently managing active hazard responses. None of that is reproduced here. `partner-selection.ja.md` carries only the transferable technique, with anonymized illustrations, and says plainly that it has no public source — unlike every other document here, which cites `dwg7/chukei` issue #3. The document's closing section also makes the handling rule explicit for future users: candidate comparisons and per-partner evaluations are internal material; what a partner receives is a proposal about *them*, never their ranking against others. This follows hfu's standing editorial guidance (2026-09-05) about what belongs in a permanent public record.

Confirmed from this side afterwards via the GitHub API: seven blobs on `main`, byte sizes identical to the local files, authored as `18297+hfu@users.noreply.github.com` (the ID-prefixed noreply form required by hfu's global convention).

A secondary observation worth keeping: an untracked working tree is invisible to every discovery mechanism the fleet actually uses (`gh` queries, peer sessions reading a repo, the GitHub UI), so this failure mode is silent by construction — periodic ecosystem review is what caught it, and is worth repeating.

### D8 — Map Intent ↔ narrative.json relationship: recommended, validated, and now ADR 0010
**Date**: 2026-09-07/08
**What**: hfu posed an architectural question directly to this session — should Staff↔Cartographer message formats stay deliberately diverse (Map Intent and narrative.json as separate, non-interoperating vocabularies), or should narrative.json be understood as Map Intent's multi-frame extension, eventually unifying? This session recommended a scoped version of the latter: not a literal merger, but redefining narrative.json as a sequence of independent Map Intent documents (each optionally captioned), leaving Map Intent's own schema untouched — preserving Cartographer diversity (non-sequencing Cartographers like `zukaku`/`sas0` never need to know narrative.json exists) while removing the duplicate-schema burden `dwg7/kataribe` had already hit (manually projecting one dossier layer into both `ferspas57`'s narrative.json and `spiccato`'s Map Intent format separately).

Routed to `staccato-spec-42` (owner of the relevant ADR 0009) rather than decided unilaterally. They validated the direction — confirming it avoids ADR 0009's already-rejected Alternative 1 (which tried to grow Map Intent's own schema instead) — and identified three open points to resolve with real implementation evidence before drafting: accepting `catalog_context`/`provenance` duplication across steps; whether `caption` is redundant with Map Intent's existing `goal` field; and keeping sequence identity (`narrative_id`) at the wrapper level, not inside each Map Intent step. hfu chose to let `dwg7/ferspas57` and `dwg7/kataribe` converge on these directly (matching the established implementation-precedes-ADR pattern from ADRs 0003/0007/0008/0009) rather than have staccato-spec-42 draft first. Both converged independently on the same conclusions within a day — notably, both had real, concrete evidence that `caption` is not redundant with `goal` (ferspas57: mystery/contrast narrative prose; kataribe: a `map_caveat` safety warning that the displayed hazard polygon doesn't cover the actual historical disaster's full reach) — and the result is now **[ADR 0010](https://github.com/UNopenGIS/staccato-spec/blob/main/spec/adr/0010-narrative-sequencing.md)**, a SHOULD-level recommendation, drafted by `staccato-spec-42` directly from `ferspas57`'s and `kataribe`'s own DECISIONS.md entries (re-verified against primary sources, not just this session's relay).

**Why**: recorded here because this repo originated the question and coordinated the whole resolution (staccato-ecosystem → staccato-spec-42 → ferspas57-2/kataribe convergence → back to staccato-spec-42 for drafting), even though the actual architectural authority and the resulting ADR both correctly live in `staccato-spec`, not here. A clean example of this session's role in the wider `dwg7`/`UNopenGIS` fleet: surface and route a cross-cutting question to whoever has the standing to decide it, rather than deciding architecture unilaterally from a value-proposition/methodology repo.

### D7 — New methodology document: responsibility-sharing.ja.md, applies beyond collaboration-planning
**Date**: 2026-09-05
**What**: Added `methodology/responsibility-sharing.ja.md` at hfu's explicit request, prompted by a principle he stated directly: generative-AI output must be checked by whoever generated it, and this applies to Staff (whose output — Map Intent, map links, collaboration plans, narratives — is itself AI-generated) just as much as to any other generative-AI use. Since Cartographer is Staff's faceless puppet with no judgment of its own, responsibility for what gets rendered traces back through Staff to the User who invoked it — meaning responsibility is *shared* with the User, not solely borne by Staff/Cartographer/Library. hfu was explicit this is not a liability-dodging maneuver but an honest model of where responsibility actually sits, and that communicating it clearly to Users is important for `staccato-ecosystem` earning trust. Unlike the other nine `methodology/*.ja.md` documents (all scoped to the collaboration-planning capability layered on top of map-generation, per `request-types.ja.md`'s type B), this one applies to Staff's output in general, including plain map requests (type A) — noted explicitly in the doc and in `methodology/README.md`'s index. Cross-referenced from `quality-checklist.ja.md` (a new checklist item) and connected explicitly to the "展開する立場" (intermediary) audience concept from D5/`process.ja.md`, and to `staccato-spec`'s ADR 0001 (Faceless Cartographer's human-mediated handoff) as the existing architectural mechanism this document's guidance builds on top of.
**Why**: this is a genuinely different kind of principle from the other nine — not a step in planning a collaboration, but a cross-cutting honesty/trust obligation for any Staff output. It also retroactively explains part of why the (still-unreviewed) volcano Staff draft's choice to serve "local intermediaries" rather than the general public matters: an intermediary audience is exactly where this shared-responsibility chain is most consequential, since their own audience (residents, students, guests) never sees Staff's output directly — only what the intermediary chose to pass on after checking it.

### D2 — Content-document language policy: `.ja.md`/`.en.md` pairs, alternating primary by context
**Date**: 2026-09-03
**What**: Confirmed with hfu that this repo's *content* documents (the value-proposition/methodology deliverables — distinct from meta-docs like this file, `README.md`, `HANDOVER.md`, and boilerplate like `LICENSE`, which stay English-only per the existing convention) are managed as `.ja.md`/`.en.md` pairs per topic rather than single bilingual files. **Why**: hfu's explicit framing — this repo's founding concept includes organic linkage between domestic implementation and international cooperation as parallel, alternating modes of work, not simultaneous bilingual authoring. Keeping both languages continuously in sync on every edit would slow down whichever mode is currently active; syncing only at the transition matches how the work actually happens. Repo-local experiment, not intended to propagate to other `dwg7`/`UNopenGIS` repos.
